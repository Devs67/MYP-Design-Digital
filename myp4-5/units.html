Unit 1 and Unit 2 — what each unit is, the concepts behind it, and the summative task.

U1

Coding for Change

Start July 22 · 19 weeks

Key conceptSystems*
Related conceptsAdaptation, Form
Global contextIdentities and relationships — lifestyle choices
Statement of inquiry“Systems are designed through adaptation and form to support human identities, relationships, and well-being by responding to changing physical, psychological, and social needs. Or systems and products can be created in different forms to support healthy lifestyle choices and improve well-being.”
GoalDesign and develop a functional multi-page HTML website that supports human identities, relationships, and well-being by responding to changing physical, psychological, and social needs.
RoleYou are a junior web designer commissioned by a youth wellness organisation.
AudienceTeenagers aged 13–16, school community, parents.
SituationMany young people struggle to access reliable and engaging information about health, transitions, and relationships. Your client needs an accessible website to support them.
Product/PerformanceA coded HTML/CSS website (minimum 4 linked pages) with navigation, media, clear layout, and researched content.
Standards for successEffective inquiry, justified design ideas, functional code, visual consistency, accessibility, usability testing, and thoughtful evaluation.
Criteria
A · Inquiring & Analysing B · Developing Ideas C · Creating the Solution D · Evaluating
U2

Designing for Everyone

Start Nov 22 · 19 weeks

Key conceptDevelopment*
Related conceptsFunction, Ergonomics
Global contextFairness and development — imagining the hopeful future (inclusivity focus)
Statement of inquiry“Through development, designers use function and ergonomics to create fair and inclusive products that imagine a more hopeful future for diverse users.”
GoalDesign and prototype a product or solution that meets the functional and ergonomic needs of a user who is often overlooked by mainstream design — such as a person with a disability, an elderly user, a left-handed person, or someone from a non-dominant cultural background.
RoleAn inclusive design consultant at a product development studio.
AudienceA community review panel including peers, teacher, and a guest representative from a disability, accessibility, or equity-focused organisation.
SituationYour studio has been commissioned by a “Design for All” initiative to create a product prototype that demonstrates how inclusive, ergonomic thinking can lead to better outcomes for everyone — not just the target user.
ProductA physical or digital prototype of the inclusive product, accompanied by: a research report, annotated design development iterations, an ergonomic and functional analysis, and a 3-minute presentation to the panel explaining how the design imagines a more hopeful future.
Criteria
A · Inquiring & Analysing B · Developing Ideas C · Creating the Solution D · Evaluating
