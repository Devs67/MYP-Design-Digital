Strand i — objective
Explain and justify that a problem is real, worth solving, and affects a specific group of people — before naming any solution.

Command terms — Criterion A, strand i

1–2StateGive a specific name, value or other brief answer without explanation or calculation.
3–4OutlineGive a brief account or summary.
5–6ExplainGive a detailed account including reasons or causes.
7–8JustifyGive valid reasons or evidence to support an answer or conclusion.

The flow

Four steps, in this order

Step 1

Design Situation

Where it happens, who is affected, and what is wrong. No solution language yet.

Step 2

User Persona

The target audience, backed by real evidence.

Step 3

Cause & Impact

The cause, and its impact at more than one scale.

Step 4

Consequence of Inaction

What happens if nobody solves this.

Cite both primary and secondary research throughout the strand, not only in step 4.

In detail

What goes into each step

Step 1

Design Situation

  • Give an overview of where this situation happens
  • State who is affected
  • Give a plain description of the problem itself
  • No solution language yet — this is what is wrong, not what to build

Step 2

User Persona

  • Describe the target audience: demographics (age, etc.), motivations, needs, values, beliefs, and attitudes
  • Back this with primary evidence — not assumptions about “people” in general
Evidence Group discussions · Semi-structured interviews · Observations

Step 3

Cause & Impact

  • State the cause of the problem
  • State its impact at local, national, and global scale
  • Support the impact with real statistics, not vague claims like “many people”

Step 4

Consequence of Inaction

  • Close by stating what happens if this problem is not solved
  • Cite both primary and secondary research throughout the strand, not just here

Four traps

The invisible audience

Statistics with no meaning

The flow

Four steps, in this order

Step 1

Map the plan

Show the plan as a flowchart or mind map before the table.

Step 2

Rank by priority

Step 3

Primary or secondary, for every question.

Step 4

Justify the choice

What you'll learn, and the decision it feeds.

In detail

What goes into each step

Step 1

Map the plan

  • Show the research plan as a flowchart or mind map before the table
  • The shape of the plan should be visible at a glance, not just buried in rows
Table columns Research Area → Research Question → Priority → Research Method → Justification

Area is split from Question deliberately — generate the broad area first, using ACCESS FM as an area generator, then write specific questions inside each area. This makes the two-step thinking visible instead of leaving it implicit.

Step 2

Rank by priority

  • Every research item gets a rank: High-1, High-2, High-3, Low-1, Low-2, and so on
  • No item sits unranked — “everything is high priority” is not a real answer

Step 3

Choose the method

  • State whether each question is answered by primary research (survey, interview, observation) or secondary research (existing data, articles, comparable products)

Step 4

Justify the choice

  • Every justification needs two parts: what information this question will give you, and how that information will help you make a specific design decision
  • A justification that only restates the question does not count

Strand iii — objective
Break down existing or similar products against a recognised framework, and make a genuine judgement — not just describe them.

Command terms — Criterion A, strand iii

1–2DescribeGive a detailed account or picture of a situation, event, pattern or process.
3–4OutlineGive a brief account or summary.
5–8AnalyseBreak down in order to bring out the essential elements or structure.

Generic IB ladder — swap in the real Year 3 task-specific clarification as soon as it is available.

The flow

Four steps, in this order

Step 1

Pick a framework

ACCESS FM, CPFC, or SWOT paired with one of the two.

Step 2

Describe

A neutral, factual account. No judgement yet.

Step 3

Analyse

What works, what doesn't, why, and what it means for your design.

Step 4

Synthesise

Sort features into Adapt / Adopt / Avoid.

In detail

What goes into each step

Step 1

Pick a framework

  • Choose ACCESS FM for physical products, or CPFC for digital products
  • SWOT pairs with one of the other two — it does not stand alone for this strand

Step 2

Describe

  • A neutral, factual account of what the product does, looks like, or is made of — no judgement yet

Example sentence

“The kettle has a stainless-steel body, holds 1.7 litres, and switches off automatically.”

Step 3

Analyse

  • Break the product down against your chosen framework and make a judgement — what works, what doesn't, why, and what it means for your own design
  • Every product needs at least one genuine strength and one genuine weakness, each tied to reasoning
  • A product with no weakness has only been described, not analysed

Example sentence

“The auto-shutoff (Function) protects users from burns, which matters directly for our elderly target audience; but the display (Aesthetics) has low colour contrast, which would exclude users with low vision — a feature to avoid.”

Step 4

Synthesise — Adapt / Adopt / Avoid

  • For each product analysed, sort its features into a three-column table
Adapt · Adopt · Avoid Adapt — a feature worth using, changed to fit your problem
Adopt — a feature worth using as-is
Avoid — a feature to leave out, and why

This table is this department's own synthesis tool, built on top of ACCESS FM/CPFC/SWOT — it bridges into the design brief, but it is not an official IB framework and should be introduced to students as such.

Strand iv — objective
Summarise the research and analysis into a brief that actually briefs something — not a re-cap of everything already written.

Command terms — Criterion A, strand iv

1–2StateGive a specific name, value or other brief answer without explanation or calculation.
3–4OutlineGive a brief account or summary.
5–8SummarizeAbstract a general theme or major point(s).

Generic IB ladder — swap in the real Year 3 task-specific clarification as soon as it is available.

The flow

Four steps, in this order

Step 1

Summarise the research

Key insights from Strand ii, concisely.

Step 2

Summarise the analysis

The products analysed in Strand iii, and what came out of them.

Step 3

Select what to carry forward

Which products and features move into Criterion B.

Step 4

Design question & statement

The line that turns a summary into a brief.

In detail

What goes into each step

Step 1

Summarise the research

  • Present the key insights from Strand ii — concisely
  • Summarise by presenting only what matters, not by leaving anything out of the earlier strand

Step 2

Summarise the analysis

  • Present the conclusions from Strand iii — the products analysed (Option 1, Option 2, Option 3…) and what came out of analysing them

Step 3

Select what to carry forward

  • Review the concerns and weaknesses raised across both strands
  • State which products and features — physical or digital — will be carried forward into Criterion B

Step 4

State the design question & statement

  • Close with a design question (“How might we [X] so that [Y]?”)
  • Add a design statement — the broad blueprint the solution will follow
  • This is deliberately not a specification (that belongs to Criterion B) — it is the line that turns a well-organised summary into an actual brief

This closing move is a department addition to the original draft — without it, the strand stops at “here's what we found” rather than “here's what we're going to do about it.” Drop it if your department's checklist does not ask for it.
