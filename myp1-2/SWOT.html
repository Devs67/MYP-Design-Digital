SWOT: Analysing Existing Products

Before you design your own poster, learn from posters that already exist — what works, what doesn't, and why.

S W O T

Learn

What Strand iii actually asks you to do, and the tool that helps you do it.

What Strand iii Actually Asks For

Before you design your own poster, you need to look closely at posters that already exist — for Ignite or similar events — and figure out what actually works about them, and what doesn't. You're not copying them. You're learning from them.

Official skill: "Analyse a range of existing products that inspire a solution to the problem."

A lot of first-attempt designs repeat the same mistakes as what came before, simply because nobody stopped to look closely at what already exists. The key word is analyse, not just look at. Saying "I like it" isn't analysis — asking specific questions and finding real answers is. That's exactly what SWOT gives you a structure for.

What SWOT Actually Means

Four angles to look at anything from — a poster, a product, an event.

S

Strengths

What does this do well?

W

Weaknesses

What doesn't work, or could be better?

O

Opportunities

What could be improved or added?

T

Threats

What outside factors could stop this from working?

A useful way to think about it: S and W look inward — at the poster itself. O and T look outward — at the world around the poster.

Weakness vs. Threat — the confusing pair

The one-line rule: Weakness comes from inside the poster. Threat comes from outside it.

WeaknessThreat
Where it comes fromInside — something about the poster itselfOutside — something in the world around the poster
Can you control it?Yes — it's a design choice, you can fix it directlyNot directly — it's a situation you design around
The question to ask"What's wrong with the poster?""What could stop it working, even if it's good?"

"If I redesigned this poster right now, would this problem disappear?"

Yes → Weakness (lives inside the design) No → Threat (lives outside the design)
Weakness "The poster is only in English." A design choice — could be changed right now.
Threat "Schools outside Hyderabad may not trust or recognise the event if they've never heard of it before." Still true even if the poster is redesigned perfectly.
A weakness gets fixed by redesigning the poster. A threat like "unfamiliar schools won't trust it" is exactly why the promotion plan and brand guidelines matter just as much as the poster — some problems can't be drawn away, they have to be strategised around.

Worked Example: Last Year's Ignite Poster

A full SWOT analysis of last year's Hyderabad-level poster.

Strengths
  • Bright, eye-catching colours that grab attention
  • Event name is large and easy to read from a distance
  • Clear date and location printed at the bottom
Weaknesses
  • All text is in English only — no regional language versions
  • Doesn't say why a student should want to attend
  • No QR code or link — no easy way to register on the spot
Opportunities
  • Could add a QR code linking straight to registration
  • Could be translated for schools in different states
  • Could explain what Ignite is for first-time viewers
Threats
  • Other school events competing for attention at the same time
  • Unfamiliar schools may not trust an event they've never heard of
  • Without wider promotion, the poster alone may not be enough

Turning SWOT Into Strand iii Evidence

A completed SWOT chart isn't the finished analysis by itself — you need to say what it means for your own design. Notice something in an existing poster, then explain how it shapes your decision.

"Because last year's poster had no QR code (a weakness), my poster will include one so students can register immediately instead of forgetting to look it up later."
