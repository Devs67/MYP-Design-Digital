Part 01

Analyse the Three Examples

Read each brief below, then write your own view underneath it — you're the one analysing now, not just reading.

How to analyse a brief

Use these questions on every example:

  1. Does the Design Statement name ONE product, or more than one?
  2. Can you point to the exact evidence in the Insight that the Statement is answering?
  3. Does the Question stay open, or does it accidentally name the product too early?
  4. What's the strongest part of this brief — and is anything missing?
Example 1 — Staying Hydrated Upper-floor classrooms

The problem, in short: students on the upper floors don't drink enough water because the fountain is far away.

InsightMy research showed that 8 out of 12 students in my class don't drink enough water during the day, because the water fountain is on the ground floor and their classroom is on the third floor.
QuestionHow might we help upper-floor students stay hydrated so that they don't need to leave class to get water?
StatementI am going to design a portable, reusable water bottle carrier that clips onto a school bag, so that students can carry water with them all day without walking to the fountain.

Example 2 — Beating the Bell Locker chaos

The problem, in short: messy lockers make students late to class because they can't find their things.

InsightIn my survey, 9 out of 15 Year 1 students said they are late to class at least once a week because they cannot find their books quickly in their locker.
QuestionHow might we help students organise their lockers so that they can find their things quickly and get to class on time?
StatementI am going to design a low-cost, foldable locker organiser that separates books, stationery, and personal items into clear sections, so that students can find what they need in a few seconds.
Your analysis of Example 2
Example 3 — Room to Focus Noisy library

The problem, in short: students avoid the library at lunch because the entrance area is too noisy to concentrate.

InsightMy interviews showed that 7 out of 10 students avoid using the library during lunch because noise from the entrance area makes it hard to concentrate on reading or homework.
QuestionHow might we create a quieter space inside the library so that students can focus on reading or homework during busy times?
StatementI am going to design a portable “Quiet Zone” divider and signage system that library staff can set up near the entrance, so that students have a calmer space to study during lunch.
Your analysis of Example 3

Part 02

Now It's Your Turn

Here's a hypothetical problem. The research has already been done for you — your only job in this task is to practise Strand iv: turning research into a design brief.

The problem

During break time, the bins near the canteen are always overflowing by the end of lunch, and rubbish spills onto the ground before the bell rings for afternoon classes.

Research findings you can use — don't add any new ones:

A number In a class survey, 14 out of 20 students said they'd seen overflowing bins near the canteen this week.
A voice A canteen staff member said: “The bins are only emptied once a day, but lunch alone fills them completely.”
A noticing Most of the overflow happens on the side of the canteen closest to the snack counter, not the lunch-tray counter.
