PEEL: Explaining Your Design Problem

A simple structure to help you explain — and start to justify — why a design problem is real and worth solving.

P E E L

Learn

When you write about a design problem, it's not enough to just say what's wrong — you need to explain it clearly and back it up with something real. PEEL helps you do that, one step at a time.

P

Point

Say what the problem is, in one clear sentence.

ExampleOne reason Ignite needs a new poster and plan is that many schools outside Hyderabad don't know the event exists.

E

Evidence

Back up your point with something real you found out — research, a survey, or something you noticed yourself.

ExampleLast year's poster was only shared in Hyderabad schools' WhatsApp groups, and no version reached other states.

E

Explain

Say why this matters. What does your evidence actually show?

ExampleThis shows that schools in other parts of India never had a chance to hear about Ignite, even if they might have wanted to join.

L

Link

Connect it back to why this problem is worth solving.

ExampleThis matters because Ignite wants to become a truly pan-India event — reaching more schools means more students get the chance to take part.

One reason Ignite needs a new poster and plan is that many schools outside Hyderabad don't know the event exists. Last year's poster was only shared in Hyderabad schools' WhatsApp groups, and no version reached other states. This shows that schools in other parts of India never had a chance to hear about Ignite, even if they might have wanted to join. This matters because Ignite wants to become a truly pan-India event — reaching more schools means more students get the chance to take part.

Practice

Now try it with your own design problem. Write one or two sentences for each part — your paragraph will build below as you go.
