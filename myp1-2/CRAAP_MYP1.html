CRAAP: Is This Source Worth Using?

Before you use something you found online in your research, run it through five quick checks to see if it's actually trustworthy.

C R A A P

Learn

Not everything you find online is worth using in your research. CRAAP gives you five quick checks to decide if a source is trustworthy enough to rely on.

C

Currency

Is it recent enough to trust for this topic?

ExamplePosted in 2015 — quite old for advice that changes fast, like event promotion.

R

Relevance

Does it actually match what you're researching?

ExampleIt's about promoting events in general, not about reaching schools across a whole country.

A

Authority

Who wrote it, and do they know the topic?

ExampleNo author name is given, so there's no way to know if they're qualified to say this.

A

Accuracy

Can you check this information somewhere else?

ExampleNo links or sources are given for any of its tips — nothing to double-check.

P

Purpose

Why does this source exist — to inform, sell, or persuade?

ExampleIt looks like it mainly exists to get clicks and ad views, not to genuinely help schools.

Example source: a blog post, “How to Promote School Events,” 2015, no author listed
C
Posted in 2015 — too old to trust for fast-changing promotion advice.
R
Only loosely related — general event tips, not reaching schools nationwide.
A
No author listed — impossible to check who wrote it or why they'd know.
A
No links or sources given — nothing to cross-check.
P
Seems designed for clicks and ads, not genuinely informing schools.
Verdict: fails 4 of 5 checks — look for a more recent, clearly authored source instead.

Practice

Evaluate up to three sources from your own research (or add more). Mark each check, add a short note, and write a conclusion for each source.

Printing only prints your name/class/date and your written evaluation below — not the Learn section above. Your evaluation isn't saved once you close this page — copy it into your workbook before you leave.
